MOSAIC

An authoring tool that turns stories into structured comics to support reading comprehension and story composition for autistic learners

MOSAIC (Multimodal Organizer for Story Analysis, Imagery, and Comprehension) is the product I build at Mosaic Cognition, where I am co-founder and CTO. It is an authoring tool that turns a story into a structured comic, making narrative structure and difficult vocabulary explicit so that autistic learners can both understand stories and write their own.

The work is written up as “MOSAIC: Visual Narrative Scaffolds for Reading Comprehension and Story Composition”, submitted to UIST 2026.

Motivation

Autistic children often struggle with reading comprehension even when decoding ability is strong. Two factors drive this:

  • Narrative — sequencing events, tracking causal relations, and inferring characters’ mental states.
  • Vocabulary — word meanings, word difficulty, and the links between words and concepts.

MOSAIC targets both. A genre-driven narrative structure makes story elements explicit, and word-level analysis lets learners simplify difficult vocabulary in place, without leaving the story.

System Design

  1. Narrative structure. The story is organized around Cohn’s Visual Narrative Grammar — Establisher, Initial, Prolongation, Peak, and Release. Each genre maps those five roles onto its own stage names (e.g. Rags to Riches: Hero in poverty → Opportunity appears → Trials and growth → Recognition or success → New life achieved). GPT-4o mini writes one segment per stage, and the draft is validated against the genre’s expected sequence.

  2. Word analysis. Every word is rated on two published norms, age-of-acquisition (AoA) and concreteness. Words outside the norms are scored by Claude Sonnet 4.6 against rated words as anchors. In the editor, hovering surfaces a word’s difficulty and clicking suggests simpler alternatives — each re-checked so that replacements are always earlier-acquired.

  3. Character extraction and customization. Claude Sonnet 4.6 extracts a character description (appearance and distinguishing features), and Gemini 3.1 Flash renders it, with the first portrait establishing a shared house style for consistency. Users can revise a character by editing the description or uploading a photo, which supports self-insertion and interest-based personalization.

  4. Panel generation. Panel generation follows the narrative structure. GPT-4o mini writes scene directions — camera framing, staging, mood — guided by each panel’s narrative role (an Establisher becomes a wide shot, for example), and Gemini 3.1 Flash renders the panels. Character portraits act as likeness references across panels, and dialogue appears in speech or thought bubbles.

What It’s Used For

Informal feedback from teachers, parents, and autistic students during development surfaced three use cases:

  • Reading comprehension and composition. Narrative structure scaffolds comprehension while the comics drive engagement. One autistic learner who had been reluctant to engage with text began composing her own stories after seeing a story broken into its structure.
  • Social preparation. Comics let a child rehearse a new situation in advance — a first day of school, a field trip — easing the impact of novelty.
  • Creative agency. A shared scaffold supports composition while open endings preserve creative choice; one teacher envisioned a choose-your-own-adventure activity where he sets the opening and students write their own paths and endings.

Future Work

We plan to evaluate MOSAIC by comparing text-only and comic-based conditions with comprehension questions ranging from literal to inferential, and to examine whether the editable structure helps learners revise text and compose their own stories over time. Ongoing development continues to strengthen privacy and safety protections, including content filtering and safeguards against recognizable intellectual property.
